Echeveria

Echeveria sp.
LightBright, direct
WaterSoak, then dry fully
HumidityLow, good airflow
FeedLight, 1–2× in summer

01 Light

Echeverias want the brightest spot in the house — ideally a few hours of direct sun in a south- or west-facing window. Without enough light, the rosette will "stretch," growing pale and leggy as it reaches for a light source, which can't be reversed on that growth.

02 Water

Use the soak-and-dry method: water thoroughly until it drains out the bottom, then let the soil dry out completely before watering again — usually every 2–3 weeks, less in winter. Try not to get water on the rosette itself, just the soil.

03 Humidity & temperature

Prefers low humidity and good airflow over a humid environment. Normal room temperature is fine; it just doesn't want to sit in damp air or a closed, muggy spot.

04 Feeding

Feeding needs are minimal — a diluted cactus or succulent fertilizer once or twice during the growing season (spring and summer) is enough. Skip it the rest of the year.

05 Common issues

  • Soft, mushy, translucent leaves — overwatering or root rot; let it dry out and cut back on watering.
  • Pale, stretched growth — not enough light; move it somewhere brighter (new growth can improve, but stretched growth stays stretched).
  • Dropping a few lower leaves — normal as the rosette matures, nothing to worry about.
Gift note Make sure this one's in a pot with a drainage hole and a proper succulent/cactus soil mix — that one detail prevents most of the problems people run into with succulents.
