Calathea Ornata

"Pinstripe" · Goeppertia ornata
LightBright, indirect
WaterKeep evenly moist
HumidityHigh (60%+)
FeedMonthly, spring–summer

01 Light

Bright, indirect light is the goal — a few feet back from an east or north-facing window is ideal. Direct sun will scorch the leaves and fade the pink and green stripes that make this plant worth having. It'll survive in medium light too, just with less dramatic patterning.

02 Water

Keep the soil consistently moist but never waterlogged — water when the top inch feels dry, usually every 5–7 days. This one is genuinely picky about water quality: tap water minerals and chlorine cause brown, crispy leaf edges over time, so use filtered, distilled, or rainwater if you can.

03 Humidity & temperature

Calatheas want humidity well above the average living room — 60% or higher if possible. A humidifier nearby, a pebble tray, or a spot in a steamy bathroom all help. Keep it between 65–80°F (18–27°C) and away from cold drafts, AC vents, or heaters.

04 Feeding

Feed with a balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half strength, once a month through spring and summer. Skip feeding entirely in fall and winter, when growth naturally slows.

05 Common issues

  • Curling leaves — usually underwatering or humidity that's too low.
  • Brown, crispy edges — most often tap water minerals; switch to filtered water.
  • Leaves folding up at night — this is completely normal. Calatheas are "prayer plants" and move their leaves on a daily rhythm.
Gift note This is the fussiest of the four, but also the showiest — if she keeps the water and humidity dialed in, the leaf movement alone makes it worth the extra attention.
